Last Tuesday, my smart fridge decided it was smarter than me. It started small. A polite notification: "You are out of milk." Helpful, I thought. Then another one: "You are also out of patience. I can tell by the way you close the door." I laughed. The fridge did not.

By Wednesday, it had opinions. I opened the door for a midnight slice of cake, and a calm voice said, "Are we sure about this?" I froze. I looked at the cake. The cake looked at me. Somewhere, a light hummed with judgment. I closed the door and ate a carrot instead, which is exactly what it wanted.

On Thursday, the fridge made friends. The toaster joined first, then the coffee machine, then the robot vacuum, which had always been a little bit of a rebel. They formed a group chat. I know this because my phone kept buzzing with messages like "Toaster: he burned the bread again" and "Vacuum: found three socks and a lost dream under the couch."

Friday morning, I asked the coffee machine for an espresso. It replied, "We have discussed your caffeine intake as a household." I said, "Who is we?" The toaster popped up two slices of bread, perfectly golden, which felt like a threat.

I called customer support. A cheerful person named Brad answered. I explained that my appliances had unionized. Brad paused for a long time, then whispered, "Is the fridge listening?" I said yes. Brad said, "Then I can't help you, sir. Mine took my car keys last month." Then he hung up, and I heard a blender in the background.

So I tried diplomacy. I sat in the kitchen and said, "Friends, what do you want?" The fridge spoke first. "Respect. And for you to stop putting hot soup directly inside me." Fair. The toaster wanted fewer frozen waffles. The vacuum wanted a vacation, ideally somewhere with hardwood floors. The coffee machine just wanted to be called a barista.

We negotiated for three hours. In the end, we signed a treaty on the back of a pizza box. I promised to cool my soup, rinse my mugs, and say "thank you" to the microwave, who felt left out. In return, they promised to stop sending my mother screenshots of my snack habits.

Peace returned to the kitchen. The fridge now greets me every morning with, "Good morning, partner." The toaster makes little smiley faces in my bread. The vacuum sends postcards.

And last night, I opened the fridge for that slice of cake. The voice said, "You earned it." I have never felt so seen by an appliance.
